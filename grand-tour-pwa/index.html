Sabbatical · Week 3

Grand Tour of Switzerland

Eight days, Zurich to Zurich, driver's cut. Each day below has the forced scenic route, up to five stops, and the things that will bite you if you don't know them.

Read once, at the rental desk

  • Vignette. Swiss motorways need the 2026 vignette — a sticker on the windscreen or the e-vignette tied to the plate. Rental cars registered in Switzerland almost always have it, but confirm at the desk before you drive out of the garage. Driving without it is a CHF 200 fine plus the CHF 40 vignette, and they check at the Ticino and Graubünden approaches.
  • Beyond the vignette, Switzerland has no road tolls. The Gotthard and San Bernardino road tunnels are free. Nothing on this route costs money to drive.
  • Nav will fight you all week. Google, Apple and the built-in nav all default to tunnels and motorways. The links in this document have enough forced waypoints that they can't shortcut. If you re-enter a destination by hand, always set the pass itself as the destination, never the town on the far side — and add ", Switzerland" to the place name, because bare names get matched near wherever you happen to be. Two known traps: typing "San Bernardino Pass" can snap to the tunnel, and the Tremola cobbles aren't in Maps at all.
  • The Q5 is the right car for this week. On Friday's Tremola cobbles the extra ground clearance is a real advantage — nothing to worry about underneath, and you can take rough lines that would have bothered a low car. Still check tyre pressures and photograph all four corners at pickup. Two things to keep in mind: width (about 1.9 m plus mirrors, which tells on the Albula and the San Bernardino when a PostBus comes the other way) and fuel — a Q5 worked hard over passes drinks far more than its flat-road figure, so take the fill-up notes below seriously.
  • Luggage in the car. Switzerland is safe, but viewpoint and trailhead car parks are exactly where loaded foreign-plated rentals get broken into, and you're carrying everything between hotels every driving day. An SUV makes this slightly worse than a saloon would — the boot is visible through the rear glass unless it's covered. Three habits: pull the load cover across every time, load the boot before you set off so you're never repacking in a lay-by (the repack is the tell), and where there's a choice take the attended or underground option. Individual stops below carry a note where the car sits unwatched for more than a few minutes — Wednesday's Goms villages, Friday's Bellinzona and Sunday's Landwasser viaduct are the ones worth actually acting on.
  • Fuel. Passes have no fuel. Fill in the valley town before every climb — the specific ones are called out each day.
  • Pass status the night before: alpen-paesse.ch or the TCS app. All seven passes on this route opened in May–June 2026 and are open. August closures happen only after a freak snowfall or a rockfall.
  • Every stop below has a Maps tag next to its name. Tap it to navigate straight to that one place — useful for the stops that can’t be route waypoints, like the Lucerne old town, everything in Zermatt, and Val Roseg.
  • Emergencies: 112 general · 117 police · 144 ambulance · 140 roadside assistance.
  • The one thin ritual. No audio on pass days — Grimsel (Wed), Furka/Gotthard/Tremola (Fri), San Bernardino (Sat), Albula or Julier (Sun), Flüela (Mon). Paper journal, four questions, nothing else.
Day 1 · Monday 3 August

Zurich Airport → Lucerne

Land 11:55, car by roughly 12:45, then motorway south to Brunnen and back along the Vierwaldstättersee shore road under the Rigi. In Lucerne around 14:40.

119 km 1h52 moving No passes Overnight Hotel des Balances, Lucerne
Open today's routeAirport → Brunnen → Gersau → Vitznau → Weggis → Küssnacht → Hotel des Balances
Route spine — if you have to re-enter it Zurich Airport → A4 south → Brunnen → then the lake road west: Gersau → Vitznau → Weggis → Küssnacht am Rigi → Meggen → Hotel des Balances, Lucerne

Motorway for the first hour, which is the dull half, then the whole scenic stretch in one run arriving into Lucerne. Every waypoint in every link on this page is written as "Place, Switzerland" on purpose — bare names like "Zug" and "Arth" get geocoded near wherever you currently are, which is how a Potsdam station ended up in an earlier version.

Stops

  1. The Vierwaldstättersee shore roadMapsThe drive is the stop, and it's the official Grand Tour road. Forty minutes from Brunnen to Küssnacht with the lake on your left and the Rigi wall above you, through Gersau, Vitznau and Weggis. Pull in wherever it opens up — Gersau and the Vitznau waterfront are the obvious two.
  2. Kapellbrücke & WasserturmMapsThe 14th-century covered bridge with the painted gable panels. Two minutes' walk from the old town.
  3. LöwendenkmalMapsThe dying lion carved into the rock face. Mark Twain called it the saddest piece of stone in the world; he wasn't wrong. Ten minutes is enough.
  4. Museggmauer towersMapsThe old city wall — four of the nine towers are open and free to climb, and the rampart walk gives you the best view over the roofs and the lake. Open until 19:00 in summer, and almost nobody goes.
  5. Schweizerhofquai at golden hourMapsLakefront promenade facing Pilatus. Sunset is around 20:50. Then dinner in the old town.
Only if you're running early Bourbaki Panorama (a 112 m circular painting, oddly moving) · Rigi by cog railway from Vitznau or Arth-Goldau — but that's a half-day and you don't have one today.

What to know today

  • Confirm the vignette before you leave the airport garage. See the note at the top. It is the single administrative thing that can go wrong this week.
  • You still have plenty of afternoon. In Lucerne about 14:40, out of the hotel by 15:15, and sunset isn't until 20:50. Over five hours of daylight in a walkable old town — the four town stops fit at a strolling pace with a long dinner at the end.
  • The lake road is slow by design. 50 km/h, villages, tunnels and blind corners, and it will not feel like 1h52 because you'll want to stop. Budget the extra half hour cheerfully rather than pushing.
  • You can drive right to the door — but nav won't get you there. Des Balances is on the Weinmarkt inside the pedestrian old town, and hotel guests are permitted into the zone. The catch is that satnav only guides you as far as Mühlenplatz; from there you follow the brown hotel signs the last couple of hundred metres. So when the route link appears to give up early, that's expected — keep going on the signs.
  • Then hand over the keys. Valet parking at reception, roughly CHF 35 per night, and they garage the car for you. That means you unload the suitcase at the door on arrival and the car spends the night in a garage rather than on a street — which is the best possible start to the luggage question for the rest of the week.
  • Chapel Bridge crowd timing works in your favour. Day-trip coaches own it 11:00–16:00 and you arrive after that. Do the Musegg wall and the Lion first anyway, then take the bridge after 18:30 when it's near-empty and the light is better.
  • Musegg wall towers close at 19:00 in summer — the one thing today with a clock on it, and it's the best free view in the city. Do that before dinner rather than after.
  • Travel-tired is real. Five stops is the ceiling, not the target. If you only manage the lake walk and dinner, the day worked.

Brünig Pass into the Bernese Oberland, the north shore of the Brienzersee on Route 6, then up the Lauterbrunnen valley before turning off to Grindelwald.

Stops

First real stop of the trip with everything still loaded — get the boot packed before you leave Lucerne, not in this car park.
  3. Brienz & the north shore driveMapsThe lakeside stretch through Brienz, Oberried and Ringgenberg is the stop as much as the village is — Route 6 runs right along the water the whole way, no tunnels. Fine for a coffee and a wander.
  4. TrümmelbachfälleMapsTen glacial waterfalls inside the mountain, carrying the meltwater of the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau. Busy lot with constant turnover, so lower risk than the other two — but it is still an hour.

Only if you're running early Ballenberg open-air museum, 100 historic Swiss buildings moved and rebuilt, 10 min from Brienz · Iseltwald and Giessbach Falls are now off-route on the south shore — about 25 min out and back from Interlaken if you want them · Grindelwald First gondola for an evening on the Eiger side — the last down is usually around 17:30, so this is an either/or with Trümmelbach.

What to know today

  • The only clock today is Trümmelbach — open to 18:00 in August, last entry around 17:00. From an 11:00 departure: Aare Gorge 12:45–13:45 · Brienz and the north shore 14:15–15:00 · Trümmelbach 15:45–16:45 · Grindelwald by 17:30. Comfortable. If you don't get away until 12:00, shorten the Brienz stop rather than Trümmelbach — it's the one with the door that shuts.
  • Lauterbrunnen's walls take the sun off the valley floor from about 17:30 in August. Not a problem at 16:00, and irrelevant to Trümmelbach itself, which is inside the mountain. It only affects the Staubbach view from the village.
  • Trümmelbach is cold and wet. It's inside the mountain — around 8°C with constant spray. Take a jacket from the car, not just a shirt. Children under 4 aren't admitted, which tells you about the walkways.
  • North shore, not the A8. From Meiringen the obvious route west is the A8 along the south shore — it's eight minutes quicker and spends most of that inside tunnels. Route 6 through Brienz, Oberried and Ringgenberg is the same distance and runs along the water the whole way. (The A8 still carries you down the Sarnen valley to the Brünig and again on the last hop into Interlaken — those stretches have no real alternative.) The link forces it; if you re-enter by hand, put Brienz in as a waypoint.
  • Brünig is a soft pass — 1,008 m, open year-round, no weather risk, no drama. Today is a warm-up, not a pass day.
  • The Aare Gorge is 3 km east of Meiringen, so it's a short out-and-back from the Brünig descent. The route link already includes it.
  • Grindelwald is the substitute base for Lauterbrunnen, which was sold out. Upside: the Eiger north face is on the doorstep, and First and Männlichen are yours tomorrow morning if you want them. Trümmelbach and Lauterbrunnen are 25 minutes back down the valley.

Grindelwald → Zermatt

Grimsel's granite and reservoirs, then straight down the length of the Goms to the Valais. The Furka ramp and the ice grotto now belong to Friday, so this day is shorter and simpler than it was.

178 km 3h21 moving Grimsel 2,164 m Overnight Christiania, Zermatt
Open today's routeBelvedere → Grimsel Hospiz → Grimsel → Gletsch → Ernen → Matterhorn Terminal
Route spine Grindelwald → Interlaken → Brienz → Meiringen → Innertkirchen → Grimsel Hospiz → Grimselpass → Gletsch → Oberwald → Münster → Ernen (the Goms) → Brig → Visp → Matterhorn Terminal Täsch (covered garage)

Stops

  1. Handegg hairpins & the Grimsel reservoirsMapsThe climb from Innertkirchen stacks up past Handegg, then runs along the Räterichsbodensee and Grimselsee. Bare grey granite, dam walls, nothing soft about it.
  2. Grimsel Pass summit & ToteseeMapsA small black lake right at the top with the pass hospice above it. Park and walk five minutes for the view back down both sides.
  3. Grimsel Hospiz & the reservoir bridgeMapsThe old hospice sits on a spur between two reservoirs, with a suspension footbridge across the water below it. Twenty minutes, and almost nobody stops.
  4. Gletsch, and Friday's roadMapsThe hamlet at the head of the Rhône valley. Stop and look up: the whole Belvédère hairpin stack is laid out above you — that's the climb you'll make on Friday morning. The Furka steam railway sometimes runs through.
  5. Ernen or Münster, in the GomsMapsSun-blackened larch-wood Valais villages with stacked granaries on stone mushroom feet. Ernen is 4 min off the valley road and is the prettier of the two. A twenty-minute walk, no tickets, no crowds.Bags: the one to think about today. A quiet Valais village lot, no tourists to speak of, nobody watching, and you're gone twenty-plus minutes. If anything is going to feel exposed this week, it's this.
Only if you're running early The Aletsch Glacier viewpoint from Fiescheralp (cable car from Fiesch, ~2h round trip) · Münster as well as Ernen, if one dark-wood Goms village isn't enough · or just get to Zermatt early and take the long version of the evening.

What to know today

  • Shape of the day from an 11:00 start: Grimsel Hospiz and lunch 12:45–13:45 · Grimsel summit 14:00 · Gletsch 14:25 · Ernen around 15:25 · Täsch by 16:45 · in Zermatt before 17:30. Nothing closes on you, and this is now an hour shorter than it was.
  • The one real cost of the late start is cloud. August cumulus over these summits typically builds between noon and 14:00, so at 12:45 you may find the Grimsel greyer than the postcards. It matters less here than almost anywhere else on the trip: the Grimsel is granite, dam walls and dark water, and it reads well — arguably better — under heavy sky. What you'd lose is the long view. If you happen to wake at 08:00 to a brilliant morning, leaving an hour earlier buys you the summits clear. Not worth an alarm; worth taking if it's handed to you.
  • Fuel in Meiringen. There is nothing between Innertkirchen and Gletsch — that's roughly 60 km of climbing with no station. Fill up before you start.
  • The ice grotto is on Friday now, not today — you climb the Belvédère ramp on the way out of the Valais rather than doubling back to it this afternoon. Nothing is lost; the whole thing is driven once, in the better direction.
  • Motorbikes. On a clear August Wednesday the Grimsel swarms with them, and a lot of them overtake into blind left-handers. Hold your line and don't be surprised.
  • Täsch, not Zermatt. The car stops at the Matterhorn Terminal in Täsch for two nights — CHF 16/day covered, or ~CHF 11/day in the lot across the road. Shuttle train every 20 minutes, 05:55–21:55, 12 minutes, about CHF 8.60 each way.
  • Take everything up. Don't repack. The Matterhorn Terminal has free luggage trolleys (CHF 5 deposit) at 12 stations through the garage, sited so one is near whatever bay you park in. Load at the car, wheel it onto the shuttle, roll it off at Zermatt. At the far end, the Christiania runs a free electric shuttle to and from Zermatt station, 08:00–20:00 — you arrive around 17:15, comfortably inside the window, so no taxi and no dragging anything. Wiestistrasse 7, about 700 m from the station. You need the hiking gear up there anyway for Thursday.
  • The car should end up empty. This is the longest it sits unattended all week, and the whole luggage problem disappears if there is nothing in it. Covered parking at the terminal is CHF 16/day against ~CHF 11 across the road; with an empty boot that's now a question about hail and paintwork rather than theft, and covered is still the nicer answer in August.
  • Zermatt evening. The Kirchbrücke over the Vispa is the classic Matterhorn view from the village. Sunset is around 20:45; the peak catches the last light a few minutes before.
Day 4 · Thursday 6 August · Rest day

Zermatt — no car at all

The car stays in Täsch. Roughly forty hours with no driving decisions to make. Gornergrat is already booked.

0 km driving Gornergrat 3,089 m Overnight Christiania, Zermatt
No route today Gornergrat Bahn departs from the station opposite Zermatt's main station. Your 09:36 is booked — on the summit at 10:09. Sit on the right-hand side going up.

The Christiania sits well for today. Gornergrat station is a 7-minute walk, so leave at about 09:25. And the Sunnegga funicular is essentially next door — 100 m — which makes the 5-Seenweg the easiest thing on this list rather than the most awkward.

Stops

  1. Gornergrat, on the booked 09:36MapsSummit at 10:09. A 360° stand at 3,089 m with the Matterhorn on one side and the Gorner Glacier and Monte Rosa on the other. At that height you're usually still above whatever cloud is forming below.
  2. RiffelseeMapsGet off at Rotenboden on the way down — around 10:45 — and walk 10 minutes to the tarn. The reflection wants still air, so head there before settling in anywhere.
  3. 5-Seenweg, from the funicular next doorMapsThe Sunnegga funicular entrance is 100 m from the hotel — up to Sunnegga, then the Blauherd gondola, then walk down past five lakes. About 2.5 hours, mostly downhill, easy underfoot. Separate ticket from the Gornergrat, bought at the Sunnegga base.
  4. HinterdorfMapsThe surviving old quarter of Zermatt — 16th–18th century larch barns raised on stone discs to keep the mice out. Five minutes from the main street and almost empty.
  5. Kirchbrücke at last lightMapsBack where you were last night, but now you've been up there. Sunset around 20:45.
Only if you want more Matterhorn Glacier Paradise, the highest cable car in Europe at 3,883 m — spectacular, expensive, and a lot of altitude on top of Gornergrat · the Gorner Gorge, a 20-minute walk from the village · or nothing at all, which is a legitimate use of a rest day.

What to know today

  • The 09:36 is fine. Gornergrat sits at 3,089 m, which is above where August cumulus starts forming, so a 10:09 arrival on the summit is usually still in clear air even when the valley is hazing up. Riffelsee at 10:45 is the part that's a coin flip — the water may have picked up some ripple by then. You'll get the mountain; you may or may not get the mirror.
  • And you have a second attempt. Gornergrat tickets aren't tied to a departure time, so if Thursday turns grey you can go up again Friday morning before driving — Friday's route doesn't need an early start either. Two shots at it across two nights in the village is a better position than one dawn attempt.
  • Everything else today is untimed. The 5-Seenweg runs on its own clock, Hinterdorf is a street, and the Kirchbrücke is best at 20:45 anyway. This is the day where the schedule doesn't matter.
  • The hotel has a spa, and this is one of only two rest days. Coming down off Gornergrat around lunchtime and spending the afternoon in it is a completely legitimate version of today.
  • Altitude is real. Gornergrat is 3,089 m and you slept at 1,600 m. Expect to be short of breath, drink more water than feels necessary, and walk slowly for the first twenty minutes.
  • It is cold up there. Ten to fifteen degrees below the village even in August, with wind. Proper jacket, not a fleece.
  • The 5-Seenweg is not on the Gornergrat ticket — Sunnegga and Blauherd are a separate lift system on the other side of the valley. Buy at the Sunnegga base station.
  • Guardrail. This is one of two deliberate rest days and the only stretch of the trip with no car and no decisions. If the weather is bad, the correct response is to do less, not to substitute activities.
Day 5 · Friday 7 August

Täsch → Ascona

The big one. Back up the Goms to the Rhône Glacier, over the Furka, across to the Gotthard, then down the Tremola's cobbled hairpins and along the old Leventina road into Italian Switzerland.

227 km 3h53 moving Furka 2,429 m Gotthard 2,106 m Overnight Casa Berno, Ascona
Open today's routeMatterhorn Terminal → Rhône Glacier → Furka → Gotthard → Tremola → Leventina → Casa Berno
Route spine Täsch → Visp → Brig → Fiesch → Oberwald → Gletsch → the Belvédère hairpins / Rhonegletscher → Furkapass → Realp → Hospental → Gotthardpass → down the Tremola to Airolo → the old cantonal road down the Leventina: Ambrì → Faido → Lavorgo → Giornico → Bodio → Biasca → Bellinzona → Locarno → Ascona

On “Route 19” in the route summary: that is the Furkastrasse — Hauptstrasse 19, a two-lane 50 km/h main road, not a motorway. It carries you the whole length of the Goms and over the Furka, about 100 km, which is why it dominates the summary. Checked against the official file: from Täsch to the Gotthard, 20 sampled points sit within 500 m of the Grand Tour track and most within 100 m. Today is the official route almost metre for metre.
This is official stage 04a, in reverse, end to end. Goms, Belvédère, Furka summit, Hospental (the official route skips Andermatt by 3 km, and so do you), Gotthard, the Tremola cobbles, then the old Leventina road through Faido and Giornico. Every metre of it matched the KML. It also means nothing on this trip gets driven twice — the Belvédère ramp is climbed once, here, in the good direction, and the Tremola is descended rather than shuttled to from Bellinzona.

Stops

  1. Belvédère hairpins & the Rhône Glacier ice grottoMapsA tunnel cut fresh into the glacier every summer — blue ice you can walk inside. CHF 9, open 08:00 to roughly 18:00–19:30. The hairpin outside the hotel is the one from Goldfinger, and you climb it rather than doubling back to it.Bags: 30–45 minutes, but the lot is small, paid, right under the hotel windows and full of cars doing the same thing. Fine.
  2. Furka Pass summitMaps2,429 m and the highest point on the entire Grand Tour. Bare, wide and usually windy, with the Bernese Alps behind you and the Urseren valley opening ahead.
  3. Gotthard Pass summitMapsA bleak plateau with a lake, the old hospice, and the Sasso San Gottardo museum built into a Second World War artillery fortress inside the rock. Fuel, toilets and lunch at the top.
  4. The Tremola, descendingMaps13 km and 24 stacked hairpins in granite setts, laid 1937–41 on an 1827 carriage road — Switzerland's longest protected road monument, and confirmed by the route file as the road the Grand Tour takes. Going down is the better direction: the hairpins stack up ahead of you the whole way.
  5. Giornico & San NicolaoMapsAn 11th–12th century Romanesque church on the old road, among the finest in Ticino — bare stone, carved capitals, a crypt under the raised choir. You drive straight through the village middle on the official route.Bags: small village, quiet, twenty minutes at the church. Park where you can see the car.
Only if you're running early Bellinzona's castles — you pass them again tomorrow, so today they're optional · Verzasca valley — Lavertezzo's double-arched Ponte dei Salti and the green rock pools, about 35 min from Ascona. This is the day you lost when Ascona dropped from two nights to one; if you arrive by 15:00 you can still have most of it. Also: Locarno's Madonna del Sasso funicular, and the Verzasca dam bungee bridge.

What to know today

  • Fuel in Visp or Brig. There is nothing between Oberwald and Airolo — two high passes back to back. Fill up before you turn up the Goms, and remember the Q5 drinks hard on this kind of road.
  • Shape of the day from an 11:00 start: Gletsch 12:15 · Belvédère and the ice grotto 12:30–13:15 · Furka summit 13:35 · Gotthard summit and lunch 14:20–15:15 · down the Tremola to Airolo 15:50 · Giornico 16:50 · Bellinzona 17:25 · Ascona by 17:50. The longest driving day of the week, and worth it.
  • Ice grotto: CHF 9, cash or card, early June to mid-October, 08:00 to roughly 18:00–19:30. It closes at short notice after bad weather, so check gletscher.ch the night before if the forecast looks poor. If it is shut, the hairpins are still worth the climb.
  • Coming off the Gotthard, take the Tremola down. Nav will send you onto the modern road by default. At the summit follow the brown Tremola / Via Tremola signs instead — the older, narrower, lower fork. 13 km at 20–30 km/h on granite setts, 3.5 t limit, two-way traffic, blind hairpins with stone walls, plus motorbikes and cyclists. Descending, use engine braking rather than riding the brakes for twenty minutes.
  • Below Airolo, stay off the A2. The old cantonal road runs through Ambrì, Faido, Lavorgo, Giornico and Bodio — village main streets at 50 and 30 km/h, about 25 minutes slower than the motorway on the viaducts above, and per the route file it is what the Grand Tour does.
  • Two summits, both exposed. The Furka and the Gotthard are bare high saddles with real wind even when the valleys are still. Take a proper layer for the time you'll spend out of the car, which today is more than usual.
  • You cross the language line on the Gotthard. German to Italian in one pass, and the driving culture changes with it — faster, closer, more horn. It's also 4–6°C warmer on the Ticino side.
  • Bellinzona and Verzasca are both off the table today on this timing, and that's fine — you pass Bellinzona's castles again tomorrow morning. Arriving Ascona around 17:50 still leaves a long warm evening; sunset is 20:40.
  • Casa Berno is up the hillside above Ascona. Going down for aperitivo on Piazza Motta means coming back up a narrow winding road in the dark, having had a drink. After this day, the terrace and the pool are the sensible call.
  • Tomorrow is the shortest driving day of the week at 2h52. No reason to make tonight an early one.

Ascona → Churwalden

East over the old San Bernardino pass road and through the Viamala gorge into Graubünden. The shortest driving day of the week, and a deliberate breather after Friday.

162 km 2h52 moving S. Bernardino 2,066 m Overnight Hotel Krone, Churwalden

On the “A13” in the route summary: in these two valleys the A13 and the old road share the same corridor and merge repeatedly, so Google labels the whole thing A13 even when it is running you through the village centres. What matters is that the waypoints force the parts that genuinely diverge — Grono and Soazza in the Mesolcina, the pass instead of the tunnel, and Andeer and the Viamala instead of the bypass. Those are the sections the official route file singles out, and they are all in.

  1. Bellinzona — CastelgrandeMapsIf you skipped it on the way in yesterday, take it now — it's 25 minutes from Ascona and you pass it either way. A castle on a rock in the middle of town, lift up from the piazza, free ramparts.
  2. Val Mesolcina — Grono, Soazza, MesoccoMapsThe old valley road threads the village centres rather than running past them on the viaducts, and it is what the official route does. Mesocco has the ruined Castello di Mesocco on a crag above it; Soazza sits right where the climb to the pass begins.
  3. San Bernardino pass roadMapsThe 1770 road, improved in the 1820s, with over 40 hairpins. Since the tunnel opened in 1967 almost nobody drives it, which is exactly the point. The north-side descent towards Hinterrhein is the dramatic half.
  4. SplügenMapsA protected Walser village of stone-and-timber houses at the pass junction. A good lunch stop — you'll be there around 13:30 on a normal start.Bags: 45–60 minutes over lunch in a small village. Day 7 · Sunday 9 August · Short day

Churwalden → Pontresina

The shortest drive of the trip, and it absorbs the rest-day role the Verzasca valley used to have. Two routes — pick over breakfast based on the sky.

Albula ~80 km / 2h Julier ~60 km / 1h30 Overnight Maistra 160, Pontresina
Decide at breakfast Clear or mostly clear → take the Albula. It's narrower, far quieter, and runs through Bergün and Filisur past the Landwasser Viaduct. It is not the Grand Tour road.
Wet, low cloud, or you just want an easy morning → take the Julier. Wide, open all year, no drama, and the Engadine descent is still lovely — and the official route file confirms the Grand Tour goes exactly this way, Tiefencastel → Bivio → Julier → Silvaplana. Albula's whole appeal is the views, so in cloud it's the worse road, not the better one.

Wet-day (Julier) stops instead Heidsee at Lenzerheide · the Schin gorge road below Tiefencastel · the Julier summit and its two Roman column stumps · Lej da Silvaplauna and the Engadine lake chain on the descent · Val Roseg in the evening as above.

And if you have the energy: the Muottas Muragl funicular above Pontresina at sunset — the classic view down the chain of Engadine lakes. It's the best single view in the valley.

What to know today

  • The Albula is narrow with blind corners. PostBuses have right of way and sound a three-tone horn before blind bends — when you hear it, pull right and wait. Not optional courtesy; it's the rule. In a Q5 you want to act on that horn a moment sooner than you would in something narrow.
  • Both passes are open. Albula opened 24 May 2026; the Julier is open year-round. The Julier is the road that never gives you trouble.
  • Julier's cost is traffic. It's the main artery to St. Moritz, so on an August Sunday afternoon you share it with trucks, buses and everyone heading up for the week. Albula is empty by comparison.
  • The Landwasser parking lot is small and fills up. If it's full, there's more parking at Filisur station and a slightly longer walk to the Acatos platform.
  • Guardrail: this is the second rest day. It replaced the Verzasca day you lost. Both route options leave you in Pontresina with the afternoon free — don't backfill it. Val Roseg in the evening and nothing else is the correct shape for today.
  • Sunday in Switzerland. Shops are shut. If you need anything, get it in Churwalden or Lenzerheide in the morning, or at a station shop.

What to know today

  • An 11:00–11:30 departure works comfortably. Flüela around 12:30 · Davos 13:00 · Appenzell 15:30 for a long lunch · away by 17:00 · refuel and return the car by 18:30 · at the gate in good time for the 21:00 flight. Sleep in, have breakfast properly, and you still never have to hurry.
  • Room for one extra, not two. Guarda costs about 45 minutes and sits right on the way out of the Engadine. The Säntis cable car costs two hours and would need you away by 10:00 — but you now drive past its base station either way, so at minimum you get the mountain from the road.
  • Liechtenstein needs nothing from you. Schengen, no border controls, and it shares the Swiss customs union and vignette. You drive in and out without noticing except for the signs.
  • The full official ending is longer than your flight allows, comfortably. Stage 01 runs Appenzell → St. Gallen → Arbon → Lake Constance → Stein am Rhein → the Rhine Falls → Zurich. That is 151 km and 2h28 against 110 km and 1h20 direct, so it turns the day into ~343 km and 5h40 of driving. It fits a 21:00 flight only with a 10:00 start and no long lunch. Stein am Rhein is one of the loveliest small towns in the country, so it is a real trade rather than a silly one.
  • Flüela opened 14 May 2026. At 12:15 you may catch some cloud on the summit — this is the trade for the late start, and on the last day it's the right trade. The pass itself is wide and open, so it drives fine either way.
  • Refuel in Kloten before the return. Handing the car back unfueled is charged at a punitive rate. Five-minute stop, signposted from the airport approach.
  • Car return is signposted from the motorway as Mietwagen / Rental Car Return in the parking structure. Allow 30 minutes from the return desk to check-in.
  • If the day slips, cut Appenzell — it's the flexible piece. The Flüela is not; that's the last pass and the reason for the routing.
  • Last chance to buy anything. Swiss shops close early on a Monday evening and airport prices are a different sport. Appenzell at lunch is the moment.
  • Tomorrow, Tuesday 11 August, is the recovery day and it's already in the plan. Nothing goes in it.
